## The one-sentence pitch

> You tell us the kind of local business you sell to. We hand you real companies,
> the right person there, and an email we've already proven will not bounce.

## The differentiator

Anyone can sell you a list. **We refuse to include a business we can't prove
exists.** Every lead traces to a Google Maps listing you can click, a real
website, and a mailbox we tested. If we can't verify it, we leave it out — which
is why our lists are sometimes shorter and always safer.

## Objection handling

**"Too expensive."**
> A dollar a lead, and every address is verified before you see it. Cheap list
> vendors charge thirty cents but a third bounce — and bounces damage your
> domain, so the cheap list costs you future deliverability.

**"I can do this myself."**
> You can. Hunter is $49, ZeroBounce is pay-per-use, and then you wire them
> together and handle verification yourself. Starter is $100 with that already
> built. Above 100 leads you're on Pro — where you *do* bring your own Hunter key
> and we just run the pipeline.

**"I already use Apollo / ZoomInfo / Clay."**
> Fair. The difference is where the list comes from. Theirs is crowdsourced and
> ages badly. Ours starts from Google Maps listings and gets verified on every
> run. Pull up our /compare page.

**"How do I know the leads are real?"**
> Click the Google Maps link on any lead. That's the actual business listing. The
> email was tested for deliverability before it reached you. And when we can't
> verify, we leave it out rather than fill the gap — that's why you'll sometimes
> see 8 instead of 10.

**"What if it doesn't work?"**
> Try 50 leads free, no card. Then cancel in one click if it's not for you.

**"Is this AI making up companies?"**
> No — and that's the whole point. Discovery is Google Places, contacts come from
> Hunter, emails are verified by ZeroBounce. AI only writes the summary from facts
> the pipeline already proved. It is never allowed to invent a business or a person.

## Demo script (5 minutes)

1. **Open their trial link.** "No login, no card. This is yours."
2. **Type their actual market.** Use their words — "commercial HVAC contractors
   in Milwaukee."
3. **Hit generate.** ~15 seconds. Talk while it runs: *"It's pulling verified
   businesses from Google Maps, finding the decision-maker, and testing each
   mailbox."*
4. **Expand a lead.** Show the grade, the signals, the source link.
5. **Click the Maps link.** This is the moment that closes — a real business,
   right there.
6. **Export CSV.** "Yours to keep either way."
7. **Scroll to pricing.** "It's on the page. Which tier fits your volume?"

**If a batch comes back short, use it:**
> That's the product working. It checked 63 businesses and threw out 24 it
> couldn't verify. A competitor would have handed you all 63 and let a third
> bounce.

## Qualifying signals

**Strong:** has a defined geography · currently buying lists · complains about
bounces · has someone doing outbound manually · agency with multiple clients

**Weak:** wants consumer leads · wants us to send email · expects 10,000 leads
for $100 · no idea who their customer is

## Current honest limits

Say these plainly — credibility closes better than hype.

- **Coverage varies by niche.** Established businesses with websites are well
  covered. Very new or web-less businesses often aren't.
- **Narrow geographies return less.** A metro area works better than one suburb.
- **We don't send email.** You export and send from your own domain — your
  reputation and your compliance stay yours.
- **Verified ≠ interested.** We prove the mailbox works. The pitch is still theirs.
